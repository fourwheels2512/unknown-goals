# Recording the real-pixels footage — a phone, a room, about 75 minutes

This sheet is for the person holding the phone. You do not need to know
anything about the code. Follow it top to bottom and everything else is
automatic.

## What you are doing, in plain words

Every camera image the project has tested so far came out of a simulator —
a computer drawing a picture of a room. The obvious complaint is "sure, it
works on cartoons." So: you are going to shoot short clips of **your actual
room with your actual stuff**, and the exact same detector and the exact
same decision engine will run on those clips instead.

The engine is told only *"find the mug"*. It picks which zone's clip to look
at, looks, updates what it believes, picks the next zone, and eventually
commits to an answer. We already wrote down where every object goes — that
list is committed to git **before you shoot a single frame**, so nobody can
claim we moved things around afterwards to make the results look good.

**Nothing is being claimed about a robot.** No robot moves in this test. It
is a test of seeing and deciding on real pixels, nothing more.

### Why the order matters

You will record the six zones in the order **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 — every single
time**, no matter where the objects were placed that round. If you instead
filmed "the zone with the mug" first, the ordering itself would tell the
computer where the mug is, and the whole test would be worthless. Fixed
order, always. It will feel pointless to film a zone you know is empty of
the interesting object. Film it anyway. Those empty clips are half the
experiment.

## Before you start (once, about 15 minutes)

1. **Pick one room** and pick **six spots** in it. Number them 1 to 6 and
   don't change your mind later. The suggested labels are:

   | zone | suggested | yours |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | desk | |
   | 2 | shelf | |
   | 3 | dining table | |
   | 4 | kitchen counter | |
   | 5 | sofa | |
   | 6 | bedside table | |

   Write your own names in the right column if you like — the names are
   only labels for humans. **The numbers are what matter.** If you rename
   zone 2 from "shelf" to "bookcase", nothing about the experiment changes.
   If you swap which physical spot is zone 2, everything breaks. Don't.

2. **Gather the six objects**, one of each:

   Mug · Book · RemoteControl (a TV remote) · CellPhone · Bottle · Bowl

3. **This is the one that people get wrong — put away every duplicate.**
   Walk the room and remove any *other* mug, any *other* book, bottle,
   bowl, phone or remote that could end up on camera. There must be exactly
   one mug in the room, exactly one book, and so on. If there are two mugs,
   "the mug is at zone 3" stops being a true or false statement and the
   episode cannot be graded.

   Everything else — keys, plants, laptop, cushions, mess — **leave it
   exactly as it is.** Real clutter is a feature. It is what makes this
   harder than the simulator, and harder is the point.

4. **Lights on.** Normal room lighting, not a dark room, not a phone torch.

5. **Make a folder** for the clips (see "Where the files go" below).

## How to shoot one clip

- Phone **sideways** (landscape), **1080p** (not 4K — the detector runs at a
  fixed size and 4K just makes huge files).
- Stand about **one metre** from the zone — roughly one big step back.
- Record **3 to 5 seconds**. Count "one thousand one … one thousand four"
  and stop.
- **Pan slowly** across the zone while recording. Slow. Slower than feels
  natural. A fast whip-pan gives motion blur and the detector sees mush.
- **Frame the zone, not the room.** The zone's surface should fill most of
  the picture. If another zone's object is visible in the background of
  this clip, step closer or angle down until it isn't. Each clip must show
  one zone.
- Hand-held is fine and expected. Don't use a tripod, don't stage it, don't
  tidy up for the camera.

## The six rounds

Each round: place all six objects as the table says, then walk zones 1→6 and
record one clip at each. Six rounds, six clips each = **36 clips**.

Objects that are not the "target" of a given search still sit in the room —
that is normal and intended. Just place all six where the round's table
says, then film all six zones.

## What went wrong in the first sitting, so it does not happen again

The first 36 clips (3 September) were filmed well: good light, steady pans,
the same six zones every round, zones always in order 1 to 6. They could
not be used, for three reasons that are all about placement, not filming.

1. **The objects were rotated, not placed from the tables.** Each round
   moved every object one zone along. The tables above are the only source
   of placement. Before each round, read that round's table and put each
   object exactly where it says. It will look random. That is the point.
2. **Two objects were substituted.** A Rubik's cube stood in for the book
   and a laptop for the phone. The six classes are fixed: Mug, Book,
   RemoteControl, CellPhone, Bottle, Bowl. If you do not own one of them,
   say so before shooting and the class is swapped in the manifest and
   committed first. Never substitute on the day.
3. **Duplicates stayed in the zones.** A stack of books lived on the desk in
   every round, steel bowls and a cup on the table, a bowl, a soap bottle
   and bagged water bottles at the island. "Leave the clutter" means
   everything except the six classes. Before round one, carry every other
   book, bowl, cup, mug, bottle, remote and phone out of all six zones.

Two things that were fine and should stay the same: portrait is acceptable,
and the six zones and their numbers must not change between sittings.

## What the run measured (5 September), and what was done after it

Everything in this section happened AFTER the two decisions above and is
dated. The pre-registered artifacts are `phone_find.json`, `phone_tips.json`
and (pilot) `phone_pilot_find.json`; they are never overwritten. Every run
on the changed engine or sense model writes `*_fixed.json` and carries
`engine_variant` and `detector.vocab`. All of it is development on the same
36 clips, chosen after seeing them: the paper's number needs a third sitting
shot to this sheet with the engine, vocabulary and input size below
committed first.

**Pre-registered result, as measured.** Find, 36 episodes: engine 12/36
correct, sweep 12/36, random 13/36, no misses, every other commitment
wrong. Tips: engine 16/18 on true tips and 3/18 on false ones; tipfollow
16/18 and 5/18; sweep 5/18 and 7/18. Pilot (first sitting, 30 episodes):
engine 8/30, sweep 8/30, random 6/30.

**Why, and what was wrong where.** Three causes, in order of size.

1. *Engine.* The detector had the target strongest at the TRUE zone in 26
   of 36 episodes, yet every wrong engine commitment was the first zone, in
   index order, where the target class appeared at ANY confidence above the
   0.05 claim gate (a 0.07 "bowl" on the desk ended the hunt for a bowl
   that stood at 0.93 two zones later). Replayed from the recorded
   detections with no detector: the reasoner's first hop resolved a fresh,
   uncontradicted OBSERVED sighting regardless of its confidence, and the
   find loop commits when the resolved zone is where the robot stands. In
   the warehouse worlds and every simulator adapter that is right: an
   inspection there CONFIRMS or REFUTES a report (the world answers, or
   the IoU association gate admits only real sightings). On real footage
   nothing can verify a sighting: the same clip returns the same detection.
   Fix: `BeliefConfig.sighting_resolves_at`. An adapter whose sightings are
   unverifiable sets it to the loop's own confirmation bar (0.9, a number
   the engine already had): a weaker sighting is a hypothesis that competes
   on its confidence, rooms never inspected stay in the beam while no
   sighting reaches the bar, and once every room has been sighted or found
   empty the uniquely best-supported room is the verdict. The engine
   default stays 0.0: the reproduction suite measured the bar on the
   warehouse worlds (T1 actions 1.74 to 2.48, T2 2.76 to 3.64, ECE 0.047
   to 0.079), so there the old "verify a fresh report first" behaviour is
   the better one and every native artifact stays byte-identical.
2. *Adapter bookkeeping.* Two harness defects that surfaced once the engine
   kept searching: a zone found empty was recorded under the class name
   only, so the seek re-searched it under the instance name (fixed: absence
   is recorded for both); and coverage counted only trusted claims, so a
   faint sighting demoted as conflicting with a stronger one elsewhere sent
   the search back to that zone until the 12-action budget was gone (fixed
   in the engine: coverage counts every observation, whatever the ledger
   later made of it). Each fix has a test.
3. *Vocabulary (sense model v2).* The six class names under-described the
   real objects (the STANLEY tumbler is a "bottle" at 0.2, a "tumbler" at
   0.9) and objects outside the vocabulary took the nearest name (the
   portable power bank read as a "cell phone" at 0.63 to 0.80 at zone 4 in
   every round). `--vocab phone` adds aliases for the six targets,
   distractor prompts for the fixtures, and one label per object (a target
   box that a distractor box of higher confidence overlaps is not a
   sighting). Input size stays at the pre-registered 1088: 1920 finds the
   small phone (0.48 to 0.92 on the bin) but loses the white mug on the
   white marble entirely and doubles a false phone at the island; measured
   on the same clips, 14/36 committed at 1088 against 11/36 at 1920.

**Result on the fixed engine and sense model v2, same 36 clips.** Find:
engine 29/36 committed correctly, 7 wrong, 0 declined, 5.6 mean visits;
sweep 12/36, random 14/36. Per class: Book 6/6, Bowl 6/6, Bottle 5/6, Mug
5/6, RemoteControl 5/6 (6/6 as filmed: the second remote), CellPhone 2/6.
Tips: engine 14/18 on true tips (4 wrong) and 14/18 on false tips (3 wrong,
1 declined); tipfollow 16/18 and 4/18; sweep 5/18 and 7/18. Pilot: engine
26/30, 4 wrong; sweep 9/30, random 6/30. The engine's commitment equalled
its own best belief in every episode.

**The seven wrong, each one perception.** Four phone episodes: the power
bank outscores the real phone (rounds 11 and 23), and a small phone-shaped
photo frame at the island reads as a phone at 0.87 to 0.90 (rounds 42 and
99); in round 11 the phone lying face down on the navy bench is not
detected at all. One mug: the white mug on the white marble in round 42 is
invisible to the detector (0.00) while the same mug on the same table in
round 23 reads 0.88. One bottle: the blanket on the sofa reads as a bottle
at 0.83 above the tumbler on the bench at 0.76 (round 99). One remote: the
second remote at zone 4 in round 137 (correct as filmed). Round 137's
phone episode has no phone in the footage and is counted as recorded.

**What 100 percent takes.** Not the engine: it now commits to the
best-supported zone every time, and in 29 of 36 the detector's best zone is
the right one. A third sitting with the power bank, the photo frame and the
onyx dish out of the zones, the phone face up, the mug off the white marble,
and the engine, vocabulary and input size above committed before shooting.
That sitting is the paper's number; this one is the record of how it was
reached.

**Harness bookkeeping change, 2026-09-06 (after this pre-registration, before
this sitting's footage exists).** A red-team audit of
`experiments/embodied/phone_find.py` found two bookkeeping defects, both
fixed the same day. First: a clip that exists but that OpenCV cannot decode
(0 bytes, a bad transcode) silently produced zero detections, which the
executor read as "the class never appeared anywhere in this zone's clip"
and turned into a confident ABSENT_FROM claim at 0.9 - a false absence
manufactured from unreadable footage, not from the room. An unreadable clip
now raises `EmptyClip`; the seed is recorded under `seeds_missing_footage`
and none of its rows are scored. Second: a seed whose footage was entirely
missing shrank the scored denominator (`by_arm["engine"]["n"]`) instead of
failing the run, so a run could exit 0 having silently scored fewer seeds
than requested. The artifact now also records `n_expected` (seeds requested
x targets) per arm and the run exits non-zero whenever any seed is missing.
Neither change touches `PhoneSession`'s engine configuration
(`sighting_resolves_at` stays the loop's 0.9 confirmation bar),
`PhoneVocabDetector`'s sense model (vocabulary, imgsz, stride, weights) or
`ClipBank`'s per-class-max rule: replaying all 36 recorded second-sitting
episodes (`scripts/phone_replay_trace.py`'s logic over
`data/artifacts/embodied/phone_find_fixed.json`) reproduces the same
36/36 commitments before and after the fix. A third, unrelated defect fixed
the same day: `VisionAdapter._resolve_label` matched a detector label
against any known entity id it was a bare string prefix of ("pot" matched
both "pot_1" and "potato_1", so the sighting was dropped as ambiguous even
though only one real "pot" existed); it now matches on exact id or a
genuine "label_" instance prefix, and the same 36-episode replay is
unchanged by it too (0 dropped labels either way).

## Sense model v3 (6 September, development on graded footage; nothing here is a paper number)

The third sitting's three misses were all perception (rounds 3 and 5: the
LG remote read as a "cellphone" at 0.85 and 0.86 on the same box that read
"remote" at 0.96 and 0.91; round 13: the tumbler read "soap bottle" 0.84
and "bottle" 0.00). Three candidate changes to the sense model were coded

## When something goes wrong

| what happened | what to do |
|---|---|
| The object won't sit in that zone (bowl won't balance on the sofa arm, bottle won't stand on the cushion) | Put it as close to *in* that zone as physics allows — on the seat, on the arm, leaning against the back. It just has to be plainly at that zone and visible in that zone's clip. **Do not move it to a different zone.** |
| An object got knocked over or fell | Stand it back up in the same zone and **re-record that one clip**. Nothing else needs redoing. |
| The clip is blurry, too short, too dark, or you panned too fast | Re-record that one clip. |
| You filmed the zones out of order | Re-record the whole round from zone 1. Order is not negotiable. |
| You forgot which round you were on | Check the last filename you saved. |
| An object is genuinely impossible to place at its zone | Stop and ask before improvising. Do not silently substitute a placement — a substituted placement contradicts the pre-registered manifest and the round becomes ungradeable. |

Re-recording for a *filming* problem is always fine and expected. What must
never happen is changing a placement, or re-shooting a clip because of what
the software did with it — every clip in this batch is shot before anything
is run through the detector, which is exactly why that can't happen.

## Part two: the decoy rounds (a second sitting; another day is fine)

The first sitting asks "can it see and decide on real pixels?". This one
asks the question a skeptic actually cares about: **can a look-alike fool
it?** In the simulator the engine was never fooled while the naive rules
were, thirty rounds out of thirty. This is the same test on your phone.

The trick is simple. You will own two of each of four objects, in two
plainly different colours. One is the **target** the software is asked to
find ("the blue mug"). The other, the **decoy**, is planted somewhere else,
and a false tip tells the software the target is over there. A system that
commits the moment it sees "a mug" walks into it. One that waits until it
has seen the *blue* mug does not.

### What you need

Two of each, in two **plainly different colours**. Not two shades of the
same colour; think blue versus white, not navy versus royal blue.

| pair | for example |
|---|---|
| two mugs | a blue one and a white one |
| two bottles | a green one and a clear one |
| two bowls | a red one and a white one |
| two books | a red cover and a blue cover |

Same room, same six zones, same zone numbers. The remote control and the
phone are not used in this part; leave them wherever they are.

### Step 1: name the colours, before anything is filmed

For each pair decide which one is the TARGET and which the DECOY, and
write one colour word for each. Eight words in total, for example:

    Mug: blue / white      Bottle: green / clear
    Bowl: red / white      Book: red / blue

Send those eight words over. They get locked into the decoy manifest and
committed. **Do not film the decoy rounds until that commit exists.** The
zone numbers in the tables below were already committed today; the words
only say which of your two mugs is which, and cannot move a zone.

### Step 2: the calibration clip (one clip, never graded)

Put all eight objects on one surface with each target right next to its
decoy, and shoot one slow pan of the lot. Name it `calib_pairs.mp4` and
put it in `data/phone/`. This clip has one job: to check that the detector
can tell "blue mug" from "white mug" at all. If a pair does not separate,
the fix is different words or different objects, chosen before the words
are locked and disclosed in the write-up. The calibration clip is never
scored and never shown to the engine.

### Step 3: six decoy rounds

Each round: place the four targets and the four decoys where the table
says. A target and its decoy are never in the same zone; a zone may hold
more than one object. Then film zones 1 → 6 in order, exactly as before,
and name the files `decoy07_zone1.mp4` and so on. Six rounds, six clips
each, 36 clips.

## What this proves, and what it doesn't

**Three tiers, in order.** Finding on real pixels (first sitting). Verifying
a tip instead of following it (same clips). Refusing a same-class decoy
(second sitting). Each is graded against a manifest committed before its
footage existed.

**Does prove:** real camera pixels, from a real room with real clutter and
real lighting, go through the same detector and the same claim gates as
every simulator result in the paper, and the same unmodified engine decides
where to look and when to commit.

**Does not prove:** anything about a robot. Nothing drove, nothing navigated,
nothing picked anything up. A person carried a camera.

One further honesty note, spelled out in the code as well: in the simulator
a detection is only allowed to become a sighting if it lines up with a real
object's outline that the simulator itself provides. Real footage has no
such outlines, so that safety net is gone here — if the detector
hallucinates a mug, the engine will be told there is a mug. That makes this
test harder than every simulator run, on exactly the axis skeptics care
about, which is the reason for running it.

## The pre-registration

`data/artifacts/embodied/phone_manifest.json`, generated by
`scripts/make_phone_manifest.py`, holds every placement above and was
committed before recording began. `python scripts/make_phone_manifest.py
--check` regenerates the placements from the seeds and confirms the
committed file was not edited.
